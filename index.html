Databricks: Lakehouse for Data & AI

A lakehouse keeps one copy of the data as open files and puts a transaction log on top that every engine trusts. Six leader sessions explain that idea and what it costs, without code: Delta tables that remember, Unity Catalog, notebooks versus jobs, the arithmetic of DBUs, and when a lakehouse beats a warehouse. Eight analyst sessions build every piece by hand on real New York taxi trips, on a laptop with no account or in Databricks Free Edition, and end on a bench that replays a real Delta log in your browser. Cluster the files by pickup zone and a JFK query reads 2 of 8 files. Cluster them by vendor and it still reads 8.

Leader track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For data and AI leaders, architects and whoever signs the platform bill. What a lakehouse is, what Delta remembers, how Unity Catalog decides access, where notebooks end and jobs begin, what a DBU costs, and how to choose between a lakehouse and a warehouse.

🏠 Leader session 1 · start here

What a lakehouse is

Three generations of data platform read from the 2021 paper that named the lakehouse, the four problems of keeping data twice, and the open questions its own authors listed.

▶ Start here 🕰️ Leader session 2

Delta: a table that remembers

Versions, history and time travel, the 30-day and 7-day retention defaults, and why VACUUM and RESTORE are governance decisions rather than engineering details.

 🗝️ Leader session 3

Unity Catalog: who may see what

One namespace for tables, volumes and models, the three keys every read needs, inheritance that reaches tables not yet created, and owners versus managers.

 📒 Leader session 4

Notebooks versus jobs

Interactive work and automated work on different compute, Lakeflow Jobs as tasks in a graph, and the checklist that turns a notebook into something that runs at 2am.

 💸 Leader session 5

What a DBU costs

The unit Databricks bills in, read from real list prices: the same five machines cost $3.53 an hour as all-purpose compute and $2.59 as a job.

 ⚖️ Leader session 6

Lakehouse or warehouse?

The paper's case, its vendor benchmark read honestly, and a decision tree for your own copy map: when one copy of open files wins, and when it does not.

the leader capstone

Analyst track 🔬 · eight sessions, one real dataset

Python on the NYC TLC yellow taxi trips for January 2024. Every build-along runs on a laptop with the open-source deltalake package, DuckDB and polars, and shows the same move in Databricks Free Edition.

🧱 Analyst session 1 · start here

Delta tables by hand

Build a two-version table from 77,033 real trips, read it with DuckDB, polars and pandas, and watch it refuse a write that would break it.

▶ Start here 📜 Analyst session 2

Reading the transaction log

Every action in the JSON, the statistics inside each add, what a remove leaves behind, checkpoints, and a short replay that agrees with delta-rs at every version.

 ⏪ Analyst session 3

Time travel and restore

Query any version, read the history, restore with a new commit, then run VACUUM with the safety off and watch an old version become unreadable.

 🔀 Analyst session 4

MERGE and change data

Apply a change feed with one MERGE, read it back from the change data feed, and meet the two traps: a duplicated source row and a NULL flag.

 🗂️ Analyst session 5

Data skipping, OPTIMIZE and clustering

Min and max per file decide what a query reads. On the whole month, 2,964,606 trips: JFK costs 31 of 31 files, then 1 of 6 after Z-order.

 🔐 Analyst session 6

Unity Catalog grants

GRANT and SHOW GRANTS as Databricks implements them, with a local model of the read rule you can run anywhere to see why SELECT alone is not enough.

 🧪 Analyst session 7

MLflow on Databricks

Track two tip models, register the better one, give it the alias @champion, and load it by alias; then the Unity Catalog names and privileges that change on Databricks.

 🎛️ Analyst session 8

The Delta log bench

A real Delta log over 8,000 trips, replayed in the browser: time travel, data skipping on your own predicate, two clustering choices and a break button.
