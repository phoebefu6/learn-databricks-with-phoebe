What a DBU costs

Every Databricks invoice is written in one unit, the DBU, and almost nobody who signs the invoice can say what one is. Tonight we take the unit apart: what it measures, why the same machine has four different prices depending on what you use it for, why a classic cluster sends you two bills from two companies, and where the money actually leaks. You leave with a monthly estimate for three of your own workloads that a finance partner can check line by line.

Part 1 · covers what a DBU is

A unit of processing, billed per second

5 min live

A DBU is a meter reading, not a machine. Each machine type is rated at so many DBUs per hour; multiply by how many machines and how long they run, and you have DBUs. Then a price per DBU, chosen by the kind of work, turns the reading into dollars. Four numbers, multiplied.

LiveWhat a DBU is, in the vendors' own words2 min▶

Databricks' pricing page defines it as "a normalized unit of processing power" that is "used for measurement and pricing purposes", metered at "per second granularity". Microsoft's Azure Databricks pricing page says the same thing in its own words: "A DBU is a unit of processing capability, billed on a per-second usage", and "The DBU consumption depends on the size and type of instance".

Read those two sentences as a leader. "Normalized" means Databricks, not you, decides how many DBUs a machine is worth; the rating table is part of the price list. "Per second" means a cluster that runs 61 minutes is billed for 61 minutes, not two hours, and also that every idle second counts. For the most common teaching machine on Azure, the DS3 v2 with 4 vCPU and 14 GiB, the rating is 0.75 DBU per hour on both all-purpose and jobs compute.

LiveSame machine, different price; Photon changes the count2 min▶

The DBU count follows the machine. The price per DBU follows the kind of work. A DS3 v2 running someone's interactive notebook on an all-purpose cluster is billed at $0.55 per DBU-hour; the identical machine running a scheduled job on jobs compute is billed at $0.30. A classic SQL warehouse is $0.22, SQL Pro $0.55, serverless SQL $0.70, interactive serverless $0.95, automated serverless $0.45. All-purpose costs 1.83 times what jobs compute costs for the same DBUs, which is why session 4 made "no schedules on all-purpose" a team rule.

Photon, Databricks' faster query engine, does not change the price per DBU; it changes the rating. The Azure page lists the DS3 v2 at 1.50 DBU per hour on all-purpose with Photon and 1.88 on jobs with Photon, against 0.75 without. Whether that is a saving depends entirely on how much faster your job finishes, and that is a measurement, not a setting.

Self-studyWhere the AWS and GCP numbers stand2 min▶

Databricks publishes its AWS and GCP price tables on databricks.com, but those tables load by script and could not be read at source for this session. Secondary pricing guides (eesel.ai and cloudforecast.io, 2026) report about $0.15 per DBU for jobs and $0.55 for all-purpose on AWS. Treat both as reported, not verified. If your platform is on AWS or GCP, rebuild tonight's estimate from your own cloud's price page on the day you use it; the four-number method does not change.

Real world

The switch that doubled the meter (constructed). A team turns Photon on for its 5-node DS3 v2 all-purpose cluster because a blog post said it was faster. The cluster's rating goes from 5 × 0.75 = 3.75 DBU per hour to 5 × 1.50 = 7.5. At $0.55 the DBU line goes from $2.0625 an hour to $4.125. Nobody measured whether the notebooks got faster. The right move was not to forbid Photon; it was to time one representative job both ways and let the DBUs per run, not the DBUs per hour, decide.

Part 2 · covers the two bills

Classic compute is two bills

6 min live

On classic compute the machines live in your own cloud subscription. Microsoft bills you for the virtual machines; Databricks bills you for the DBUs those machines consume. Two invoices, often two cost centres, one cluster. Here is one hour of the same 5-node DS3 v2 cluster, priced both ways.

LiveTwo invoices, in Azure's words2 min▶

Microsoft states it plainly: "Azure Databricks bills you for virtual machines (VMs) provisioned in clusters and Databricks Units (DBUs) based on the VM instance selected." A DS3 v2 Linux VM in East US lists at $0.293 per hour in the Azure retail prices API. Five of them cost $1.465 an hour whatever they are doing. On top of that sit the DBUs: 3.75 an hour for five nodes, priced at $0.55 on all-purpose ($2.0625) or $0.30 on jobs ($1.125). Total: $3.5275 an hour or $2.59 an hour.

Notice what does not move. The VM line is identical in both rows, because the machines are identical. The workload type only reprices the Databricks half. That is the reason a finance partner reading only the Databricks invoice will underestimate classic compute, and a cloud team reading only the Azure invoice will not see why the Databricks one doubled.

LiveServerless: one price, Databricks' machines2 min▶

Serverless compute runs "in the serverless compute plane, which is managed by Databricks", not in your subscription, and is priced per DBU at its own rates. A serverless SQL warehouse is sized in DBUs per hour on the Azure page: 2X-Small 4, X-Small 6, Small 12, Medium 24, Large 40, X-Large 80, 2X-Large 144, 3X-Large 272, 4X-Large 528. The smallest, at 4 DBU per hour × $0.70, lists at $2.80 an hour.

Databricks' classic compute best-practices page says "For most new workloads, Databricks recommends using serverless compute." Note who is recommending. Serverless removes the second bill and the idle machines you forget, and it carries a higher price per DBU. Whether it is cheaper for you depends on how much of your classic cluster time is idle, which is Part 3.

Self-studyThe cloud side of the bill2 min▶

Tonight covers the Databricks side: DBUs and the VMs that classic clusters add. The rest of the cloud bill, storage for the Delta files, network egress, reserved capacity and how to read a cloud invoice, belongs to the cloud itself and is covered in Learn AWS with Phoebe. Storage for a lakehouse is real money too; it is simply not metered in DBUs.

Real world

The half of the bill nobody owned (constructed). A finance partner reconciles the monthly Databricks invoice against the platform team's budget and finds it on target. Three months later the cloud team asks why the Azure subscription that hosts the data platform is over budget. Both teams were right about their own invoice. The VM lines for every classic cluster sat in the Azure bill, under a cost centre nobody connected to Databricks. The fix was one sentence in the budget template: classic compute is two bills, and both lines are owned by the same person.

Part 3 · covers where money leaks

Idle is the expensive part

5 min live

Per-second billing cuts both ways. A two-hour job costs two hours. A cluster that nobody is using, left running overnight, also costs every second it stays up. Here is one constructed day for the same 5-node DS3 v2 cluster.

LiveTurn idle off2 min▶

Databricks' classic compute best-practices page is direct: "Enable auto termination to ensure compute is terminated after a period of inactivity." An all-purpose 5-node DS3 v2 cluster that sits idle for 14 hours overnight costs 14 × $3.5275 = $49.39, against $7.06 for the two-hour job that actually needed it. The job is not the expensive part. The hours nobody is watching are.

Jobs compute ends with the job by design: the cluster starts for the run and stops when the run finishes. Serverless removes idle machines from your side of the ledger. The cluster most at risk is the shared all-purpose one that analysts start at 8am and nobody owns at 6pm.

LiveThe 1.83 ratio, over a month2 min▶

All-purpose DBUs cost 1.83 times jobs DBUs ($0.55 against $0.30). Once the VMs are added the gap narrows to $3.5275 against $2.59 an hour, because the VMs cost the same either way. Over 30 nights the same 2-hour job costs $211.65 on all-purpose and $155.40 on jobs compute. That is the money session 4's promote-a-notebook checklist saves, and it is the smaller leak: one idle night of the analysts' cluster costs almost as much as that whole monthly difference.

Self-studyFree Edition: no bill, but a quota1 min▶

Databricks Free Edition is free, for non-commercial use, and serverless only: no custom compute, and one SQL warehouse limited to 2X-Small. It has no invoice to estimate, but it has a quota. The limitations page says an overrun shuts compute down "for the rest of the day (and in extreme cases, the rest of the month)". The habit is the same one that saves money on a paid workspace: stop what you are not using. Limits change, so re-read the Free Edition limitations page before relying on any of them.

Real world

The long weekend (constructed). An analyst starts the team's 5-node DS3 v2 all-purpose cluster on Friday afternoon, runs one last query at 6pm, and goes home for a long weekend. Auto-termination was switched off months ago because "it kept killing my session". The cluster runs until someone notices on Monday at 6pm: 72 idle hours × $3.5275 = $253.98 at list price, for no work at all. The fix was not a reprimand. It was turning auto-termination back on as a workspace policy and making the short restart the accepted cost of a shared cluster.

Your monthly estimate

List three workloads with their hours per month. One scheduled job, one interactive cluster, one SQL warehouse that serves dashboards. For each, write the compute type, the machine and node count, and the hours it actually runs in a month, not the hours someone is working.

Price the nightly job on jobs compute. DBUs first: nodes × rating × hours, then times the jobs rate. Then the VMs: nodes × VM price × hours. Add the two bills.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and leader session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · The same 5-node DS3 v2 cluster costs $3.53 an hour as all-purpose compute and $2.59 as jobs compute. Why?

Same machines, same DBU rating, same VM line. Only the Databricks rate changes with the kind of work: 3.75 DBU × $0.55 = $2.0625 against 3.75 × $0.30 = $1.125, each plus $1.465 of VMs.

2 · What does turning Photon on do to the DBU count of a DS3 v2 node?

Photon raises the rating per hour. It can still cost less per run if the run finishes fast enough, which is why the decision belongs to a timed test on your own job, not to a default.

3 · A 5-node DS3 v2 all-purpose cluster is left on with nobody using it for 14 hours overnight. At the list rates above, what does that cost?

A running cluster is billed every second it is up, whether or not anyone is using it. Auto-termination is the cheapest control on the platform.
